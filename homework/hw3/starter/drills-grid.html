Each drill links the page it needs. Check your answer against that page's table: the CSS column tells you what you actually asked the browser for.

Window width: — drills 6 and 7 change their answer at 768 px. Resize and they should stay green on both sides.

1. Three columns

docs: grid-template-columns

Six boxes, three across. They will wrap onto a second row by themselves.

2. Space the cells apart

docs: gap

The boxes are touching. One class separates them both across and down.

3. Two columns instead

docs: grid-template-columns

Same six boxes, two across, three rows. Change one number.

4. One box, two columns wide

docs: grid-column

The grid stays at three columns. Make the dark box take up two of them. This class goes on the box, not on the container — the only drill where that is true.

5. One box, two rows tall

docs: grid-row

Same idea, the other direction. Make the dark box span two rows.

6. One column on a phone, three on a laptop

docs: responsive design

This is Task 2 of the homework. Write the phone layout with no prefix, then add the wide-screen one with md:. Resize the window past 768 px to check both.

7. Article and sidebar

docs: grid-column

Task 3 of the homework. Stacked below 768 px. At 768 and up, three columns with the dark article taking two of them and the sidebar taking one. Two classes: one on the container, one on the article.
